# Aurevost

## The world runs on a miracle that may be eating it.

Aurevost is an ocean world of crowded ports, compact nations, airship gantries,
old shrines, and industrial magic. Three centuries ago, the First Setting made
arcane power repeatable at civic scale. Khevra now runs the lamps, lifts,
foundries, message-glass, ships, weapons, and prestige airships that hold modern
life together.

On the day khevra changed the world, reliable divine communion failed.

Most people decided the gods had stepped aside because mortals had grown up.
Others are less certain. Refinery workers sicken slowly. Living places recoil
from active setwork. Old devotional traces dim near loaded khevra. A militant
revival movement says the modern world is poison. Powerful institutions answer:
maybe, but the elevators still need to run and the food still needs to move.

The campaign begins small. You are nobodies in Caelport, a vertical port city of
sea docks below and airship gantries above. In the Undercroft, a cramped tavern
called the Brass Lantern shares a wall with a bricked-over shrine. Behind it, a
single ember has burned since the First Setting. A quiet stranger has started
asking about old devotional objects.

You can ignore the grand crisis for a long time, maybe forever. Hunt veined
beasts near khevra deposits. Smuggle relics through sea fog. Take Concord
bounties. Crew an airship. Protect a shrine. Sell a core to the wrong buyer.
Uncover why every faction's evidence is incomplete.

Aurevost does not require an end-the-world campaign. It supports seafaring
high-magic adventure, faction politics, khevra research, shrine mysteries, trade
jobs, bounty work, and ordinary D&D danger. The deepest truth exists for tables
that keep digging; the world is still real if they never find it.

If khevra is harmless, the world is messy but survivable.

If khevra is wrong, civilization is built on the thing that is killing it.

And if the world must step away from khevra, someone has to decide who pays for
the transition.
