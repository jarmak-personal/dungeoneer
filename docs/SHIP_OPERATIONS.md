# Ships Between Battles

## Purpose

A ship is a place the party travels and works in, an asset with obligations,
and a means of choosing where to go. It is not automatically a party-owned
vehicle or a combat encounter. This document connects ordinary voyages to
[Spatial and travel](SPATIAL_TRAVEL.md) and
[Ship combat](SHIP_COMBAT.md); neither the ship nor its crew makes decisions
for the PCs.

The rules pack and ship content determine mechanical capacity, costs, durations,
damage, and repairs. This document defines the choices and evidence a voyage
needs; it does not invent numerical ship stats, prices, or new actions when
their definitions are missing.

## Obtaining A Voyage Or Vessel

A party can buy, charter, borrow, crew, or bargain for passage on a vessel.
Before commitment, establish the vessel, who controls it, the proposed route,
passengers and cargo, the party's authority, known constraints, price or other
consideration, and what happens if plans change. A passenger may choose whether
to board but does not automatically command the captain. A charter's scope is
whatever its accepted agreement says, not ownership by implication.

For purchase, establish seller authority, price, transfer conditions, and
whether debts or other claims are known. Money and custody change only when the
agreement's conditions are met and the normal transaction validates. If
ownership, permission, or a required value is uncertain, surface that
uncertainty; the DM must not silently settle it in favor of either party.

## Crew, Supplies, And Decisions

The ship profile specifies capacity and any required crew, equipment, fuel,
and supply range. An agreement identifies the captain, crew, pay, duration,
and roles when those matter. A crew member is an actor with their own
knowledge, limits, and interests, not a resource the party can spend as if
they had consented to everything. Routine crew in ship combat can use the
pooled crew model; named people and their individual choices remain distinct.

Before departure, show the party the known itinerary, costs, available
supplies, and foreseeable risks. Players choose what they control. A hired
captain or crew can accept, counter, decline, or warn about an order according
to their agreement and perspective. For PC-controlled stations, the relevant
player chooses that actor's action. The captain can settle shared ship
movement, not dictate another player's station action.

If a needed ship profile, crew requirement, supply consumption rate, or other
mechanical rule is missing, return the existing non-mutating blocked rules
result. Do not invent a number or treat missing supplies as free. A scene can
still proceed without those values when it does not depend on them.

## Operating A Voyage

Plan using the selected vessel's travel profile and known route. Record
departure, occupants, cargo, itinerary, agreed roles, relevant resources, and
assumptions. Travel events advance the voyage and world time; an estimate is
not arrival. Summarize uneventful spans and open a scene when an established
event gives the party a consequential choice. See
[Adventure play](ADVENTURE_PLAY.md#travel).

If an incident interrupts the trip, record where and when it interrupts, why,
and the ship's known state. The party can negotiate, stop, divert, resist, or
withdraw where those actions are possible. If combat starts, preserve the
travel event and open a separate tactical encounter using an authorized
starting scope. At its end, apply its accepted consequences, then either
resume the original journey with updated assumptions, choose a new route, or
remain stopped. The encounter does not automatically complete the travel plan.
The exact event payload linking a particular journey to a ship engagement is
still an open contract; a worked mechanical handoff must specify it before
claiming replay is complete.

## Cargo, Wear, And Repair

Record significant cargo's custody, destination, delivery terms, and known
restrictions. A delivery is complete only when its agreed conditions are
established. Inspection, damage, loss, salvage, and claims are possible
consequences, not automatic outcomes of crossing a border.

Hull damage, installed components, and other rules-owned ship state change
through sourced mechanical consequences. Narrative descriptions cannot repair
HP or create an intact component. Before repair, establish who is authorized,
the required definition, materials, location, time, and cost; if any required
mechanical definition is absent, block that repair rather than inventing the
amount restored. A captain may negotiate an alternative service or postpone
departure. Operating expenses and crew pay are commitments only if established
by pack rules or agreements, not invented debt at the end of a scene.

## Worked Voyage: Relic Passage

Use the existing `thread:the_ember_must_move` only when its unlock conditions
have actually been met. Tovin asks the party to arrange discreet passage for
the Emberglass; the party may refuse or seek another plan. A captain is willing
to discuss carriage, but neither a vessel, fee, route, nor agreement is
presumed. The party can ask about privacy, custody during the voyage, risks
from inspection, arrival, and whether the captain has the capacity to carry
the relic safely.

If the parties agree, the accepted terms determine who has custody and what
delivery requires. Route options come from authorized travel data and the
characters' knowledge. If that data or a ship profile has not been authored,
the voyage requiring it is blocked rather than filled in with invented
distances. The party can choose a different authorized mode, wait, or
renegotiate. A later inspection becomes a playable scene only if it arises from
an established event; the ship's crew, inspectors, and the PCs do not
automatically know the relic's secret.

## Remaining Authoring Work

- Ship profiles, purchase and charter terms, crew requirements, vessel
  maintenance and repair definitions, supply consumption, and upgrades.
- The ship-combat action catalog, crew assignment and replacement, and
  movement, collision, and range rules listed in
  [Ship combat](SHIP_COMBAT.md#settled-vs-open).
- A replayable travel-to-encounter-to-travel event and worked instance using an
  authored route and ship, rather than a route inferred from lore.
