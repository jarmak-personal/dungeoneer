# The Greywake Cull

## What This Is

A worked opening-tier Aurevost job for one or more PCs. It exercises
[Adventure play](ADVENTURE_PLAY.md), the khevra creature and harvest rules,
and ordinary NPC, item, time, and consequence boundaries. The names, prices,
route durations, and local details below are **scenario fixtures**, not new
facts about the shared world seed. They become campaign canon only through
normal import or accepted events.

The party can negotiate, refuse, take another patron's work, spare the animal,
or leave. Do not use the example to force the Hearthward plot or determine
what a particular campaign does.

## Opportunity And Terms

At Caelport's Waterline, an independent boat operator, Mara, reports a veined
wolf near an abandoned loading landing. Workers have seen an injured animal
and fear approaching the old storehouse. Mara offers the party 30 gp for
making the landing safe, payable on her reasonable confirmation that the
threat no longer prevents access. She does **not** buy or claim a core under
this offer. She can promise only her own payment, not a Concord license.

What the party can learn without a roll by asking Mara: the last sighting was
yesterday, a skiff can reach the landing, and she has not examined the animal.
She does not know whether it attacks unprovoked or whether a core is intact.
The PCs can question the workers, seek local licensing advice, bargain for
different terms, or decline. Treat licensing requirements not stated in this
fixture as unknown; a patron's confidence does not establish law.

The cull offer, the core's custody, and any later sale are separate agreements.
Mara accepts a genuinely secured landing as completion, including nonlethal
removal if she can reasonably verify it; the party need not kill the wolf.
If the party refuses, the job remains an opportunity, not a failed quest.
For settlement in this fixture, Mara has 30 gp available, the skiff operator
has 0 gp, the buyer described below has 20 gp, and Dorn starts with 2 gp. For
this worked scenario Dorn consents to pay the fare and receive the commission
and core; actual campaigns use their accepted character and party balances.
These are fixture assumptions, not ongoing guarantees about those NPCs.

## Getting There

The example assumes the party can arrange a small skiff. The known water route
takes about two hours each way in normal conditions. The fee is 2 gp round trip
for this fixture, agreed before departure; it is not a universal fare. An
alternative overland approach takes about four hours each way, crosses a muddy
causeway, and does not require the skiff fee. Weather, danger, and additional
transport are not invented after the choice merely to punish it.

The party chooses route and timing. A calm, uneventful trip can be summarized.
If an established storm or inspection interrupts it, open a scene and give the
party its available choices. Arrival advances time by the actual chosen route;
turning back does not imply arrival or automatically complete the commission.

## Landing

The landing has three connected areas:

- **Jetty:** an open approach with damaged crates and recent paw marks. From
  here the storehouse entrance and a narrow path around it are visible.
- **Storehouse:** a roofed room with a loading door and a rear opening. Broken
  lattice-bearing equipment lies inside. Moving carefully through it is
  possible without a roll; disturbing unstable material recklessly is a risk,
  not an automatic injury with invented damage.
- **Reed bank:** a sheltered way around the building. A veined wolf rests here
  near its den. It is injured and defensive, not automatically hostile. The
  party can see the veins from an appropriate vantage without a check.

The wolf is the cause of the workers' concern, not proof it caused every
damaged crate. The party may observe, withdraw, try to drive it away, seek help,
or fight. A credible calm or diversion can avoid a fight; resolve uncertainty
through existing checks and the animal's established behavior. The fixture
does not promise that any one approach works regardless of what happens.

## Identification And Combat

Use `rules:creature.wolf` from the active pack and add the khevra-infused
template. This fixture chooses the +1 AC edge: the wolf has AC 14 and, because
it is injured, 8 of 11 HP when encountered. Its attack, speed, and other traits
come from the base stat block.
For this worked example the wolf has proficiency +2 and Constitution modifier
+1; its stability check is therefore +1 against DC 12 and a blast save is
Dexterity DC 11 against 2d6 force damage in 10 feet. If the required base
stat block or another needed rule is not available, return a blocked rules
result; this example does not authorize inventing the missing 5e mechanics.

Before defeat, a character with a chance to examine the wolf may identify the
lattice using an appropriate Arcana, Nature, or Survival check against DC 12.
A success explains the danger of a discharge and the relevant careful-hunting
choices, and grants advantage on the first defeat stability check. A failed
identification does not remove visible veins or prohibit a different source
of information. The GM should describe foreseeable danger to a character who
already knows it, not require a needless check for common knowledge.

On the first reduction to 0 HP, resolve the template's stability check.
Eligible nonlethal melee defeat also grants advantage; critical or
force/lightning/thunder finishing damage imposes disadvantage. The pack's
roll-shape policy cancels advantage against disadvantage. A successful check
leaves the lattice active and the core intact; it has not yet been secured.
Failure triggers the blast,
degrades the core one grade, and discharges the lattice. Resolve the blast
before the next action.

Worked branch: identification succeeded, the finishing hit was ordinary, and
the wolf rolls 7 and 14 with advantage. The chosen 14 plus 1 is 15, so the core
remains stable and intact. If the chosen die had been 7, the total 8 would
fail; every affected creature would make the DC 11 Dexterity save and the core
would become degraded. The natural 1/20 rule applies to the chosen die.

## Harvest Or Another Outcome

The party may leave the animal alone, transport it, seek specialist help, or
harvest only when the procedure's preconditions are met. The harvest and
stabilization procedures require a dead creature; nonlethal defeat does not
automatically satisfy them. Live-animal extraction is not defined.

An optional separate one-minute stabilization check can secure an active core
before harvesting it. Success protects against a later ordinary fail-band
harvest triggering a stability check; botch and reckless damage remain
dangerous. Failure calls for a stability check and can cause a blast. A
character may choose, but is not required, to use druidic channeling; that
choice risks exposure. Stabilization cannot repair core quality.

The harvest procedure uses DC 11 for this wolf: `8 + 2 proficiency + 1
Constitution`. Careful work takes ten minutes; rushed work takes one minute
and raises the DC to 16. Identification and tools can grant advantage under
the procedure; they do not stack as multiple advantages. On a success, an
intact core stays intact. A previously degraded core stays degraded
even on a strong result. A failed harvest caps the grade at degraded, then
checks stability if the lattice is active rather than secured or discharged.
A failed stability check causes the blast and degrades the resulting core by
one further step.
After the consequences resolve, the completed harvest transfers the core from
the wolf to Dorn, the chosen recipient in this fixture. Mara agreed that the
core is not part of her commission; in this fixture no other claim restricts
the party's authority to sell it. Other campaigns must establish their own
ownership, claims, and applicable law rather than assuming this outcome.

The procedure owns contamination. The example does not create exposure merely
for being nearby or for a sealed core. If druidic stabilization is used, apply
the condition's defined triggers and outcomes; do not turn it into an
unrequested power or ignore the player's choice.

## Return, Sale, And Consequences

Mara verifies the landing if she can safely do so. If the wolf is gone or no
longer prevents access, she pays the agreed 30 gp; a core is not needed to
collect that payment. If the animal remains a danger, she can decline payment
under the agreed terms and explain why. An altered agreement is negotiated, not
silently imposed.

For the example, an independent buyer offers 20 gp for an intact core, 5 gp for
a degraded one, and nothing for an inert one. These are this buyer's offers,
not canonical market prices or a guarantee of legality. The party may sell,
keep, surrender, or refuse to harvest it. Transfer payment and item custody
through the normal typed paths. The buyer knows only what they actually observe
or are told; purchase alone does not establish a faction conspiracy.

The return trip consumes its actual time and can be interrupted only for a
reason established in play. A fulfilled promise, payment, core transfer, or
meaningful relationship change carries its source. Broader consequences
(buyer interest, later rumors, or faction attention) need a concrete source
and use live/deferred/review routes; a core sale does not automatically advance
an unrelated clock. The active campaign's advancement policy decides any XP
or milestone progress.

## Questions This Fixture Should Survive

- Can the party complete the job without killing or selling a core?
- Can it decide not to take the job and still continue play?
- Does identifying the wolf before defeat affect stability without retroactively
  changing the result?
- Are a discharged core and a degraded core kept distinct?
- Can a failed harvest worsen but never improve the current core grade?
- Are the patron's fee, the skiff fee, and the later sale separate agreements?
- Can the party leave, rest, return, or choose another buyer without a hidden
  quest script forcing the next scene?
